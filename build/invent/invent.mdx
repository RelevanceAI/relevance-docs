## What is Inventor

Inventor is a forward deployed agent inside the Relevance AI UI. Rather than doing every job itself, it staffs a team of specialist agents and manages them: it learns your processes, connects the systems those processes run on, builds an agent for each job, sets the quality bar each one has to pass, then monitors, fixes, and measures the results.

It uses the Relevance AI MCP under the hood to take real actions on your project, so from a single chat surface you can build, debug, and iterate without leaving the platform.

Give Inventor to a single leader, a team, or every individual contributor — it builds and manages the agents underneath it in each case.

## Where to find Inventor

Inventor is available as a persistent side panel from any builder page — tool, agent, workforce, knowledge, and home. The universal side panel is the primary way to access Inventor.

<Info>
  The standalone `/invent` route is being deprecated. Use the side panel instead.
</Info>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Side panel">
    The Inventor side panel is accessible from any page in the Relevance AI builder. Look for the **Inventor** button in the left sidebar or page header — clicking it opens the panel without navigating away from your current page.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Home page">
    <img src="/images/invent-dashboard-access.png" alt="The Inventor home page and sidebar access point in Relevance AI" />

## Credit usage

<img src="/images/invent-credit-counter.png" alt="The real-time credit counter in the Inventor panel" />

The Inventor panel shows a real-time credit counter — "X credits used" with a coins icon — that updates with each interaction, such as generating an agent, requesting an adjustment, or asking a follow-up question.

The counter lives in the Inventor panel and tracks only the credits used in your current session.

## Inventor features

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Auto-accept and tool approval">
    <img src="/images/auto-accept.png" alt="The auto-accept setting in Inventor" />

    By default, the changes Inventor makes land in draft so you can review and approve them before they go live. Turn on auto-accept to let Inventor apply its changes as it works, without stopping for your approval. Version restores are the exception — Inventor always asks you to confirm before restoring. Keep it off when you want a checkpoint on each change, and turn it on when you trust Inventor to iterate quickly.

    When auto-accept is off, tool runs that require your approval appear in the approval queue. The queue shows pending actions and lets you approve or reject each one before Inventor proceeds. This gives you fine-grained control over exactly what Inventor executes.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Model selection">
    <img src="/images/invent-model-selector.png" alt="The model selector in Inventor" />

    The model dropdown sets which LLM powers your Invent session. Models are grouped into three tiers:

    <CardGroup cols={3}>
      <Card title="Cheapest" icon="piggy-bank">
        Cost-optimized models. Free plan users default to this tier to preserve credits.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Balanced" icon="scale-balanced">
        A middle ground between cost and capability. Includes models like Claude Sonnet 4.6.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Most capable" icon="brain">
        Highest-capability models for complex tasks. Includes models like Claude Opus 4.8.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>

    Your plan determines the default model. You can switch models at any time from the dropdown in the Inventor panel.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Memories">
    Inventor automatically learns about you across conversations and stores what it learns as memories. You don't add memories manually — Inventor builds them passively as you work.

    Memories let Inventor carry context between sessions: your role, the kinds of things you build, naming conventions you prefer, and other patterns it picks up over time.

    To view, search, edit, or delete your memories:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open Inventor settings">
        Click the settings icon in the Inventor panel header, or open **Memories** from the sidebar.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Manage your memories">
        Browse the list, search for a specific memory, edit its content, or delete memories you don't want Inventor to carry forward.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Info>
      Deleting a memory removes it permanently. Inventor will re-learn that information if it comes up again in future conversations.
    </Info>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Scheduled check-ins">
    You can schedule recurring Inventor conversations so Inventor checks in with you at regular intervals — useful for standing reviews, periodic status summaries, or recurring build sessions.

    To set up a scheduled check-in:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the overflow menu">
        In the chat toolbar or task board header, click the overflow menu (three dots).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Select Scheduled check-ins">
        Choose **Scheduled check-ins** from the menu.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Configure the schedule">
        Set the recurrence, time, and what you want Inventor to do at each check-in.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Voice sessions">
    Voice sessions let you describe what you want to build by speaking rather than typing. Inventor listens, processes your request, and responds — then when you're done, you conclude the session and Inventor builds from the conversation.

    To start a voice session:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the voice session">
        Click the microphone icon in the Inventor panel.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Describe what you want">
        Speak your request. Inventor shows a breathing animation while it listens and processes.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Conclude and build">
        When you're finished, click **Conclude**. Inventor transitions from the voice interface and applies your request.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Subagent spawning">
    For complex workflows, Inventor can spawn child agents to handle parallel tasks. Instead of executing steps sequentially, Inventor fans out work across multiple subagents and coordinates their results.

    When Inventor spawns subagents, the panel shows the status of each child agent — running, completed, or failed — so you can follow along as the work progresses. Results are collected and surfaced back to you in the main conversation once all subagents complete.

    This happens automatically when Inventor determines that a task is better handled in parallel. You don't need to configure it manually.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Suggested actions">
    When you open a new Inventor conversation, the empty state shows suggested actions to help you get started. The suggestions are context-aware — they change based on what you're working on.

    When you have an agent open:

    <CardGroup cols={2}>
      <Card title="Add test coverage" icon="flask-vial">
        Suggested when your agent doesn't have evals set up.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Add production monitoring" icon="chart-line">
        Suggested when your agent doesn't have a monitoring dashboard.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Summarize test runs" icon="list-check">
        Suggested when your agent has existing eval runs to review.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Summarize performance" icon="gauge">
        Suggested when your agent has monitoring dashboards with data.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>

    Without a specific agent context, Inventor shows general starting points:

    <CardGroup cols={1}>
      <Card title="Build from a discussion" icon="comments">
        Start by talking through what you want to build before Inventor generates anything.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Discuss best practices" icon="book-open">
        Ask Inventor to walk through patterns and approaches for your use case.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Improve reliability" icon="shield-check">
        Get Inventor to audit an existing agent or tool for reliability gaps.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="About you">
    <img src="/images/about-you-invent.png" alt="The About You setting in Inventor" />

    About You tells Inventor who you are so it tailors every response to your role and work from the first message — no re-explaining your context each session. It's descriptive only: it shapes how Inventor talks to you and what it assumes about your work, but it doesn't change Inventor's role or hardcode any tool configuration.

    What it changes:

    <CardGroup cols={3}>
      <Card title="Tone and language" icon="comments">
        Simpler explanations for non-technical users, more precise technical language for engineers.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Domain assumptions" icon="lightbulb">
        If you're in sales, it won't default to engineering-flavored tool designs.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Personalization" icon="user">
        Addresses you by first name at natural moments like greetings, not robotically in every message.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>

    To set it up:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open Inventor">
        Open the Inventor side panel or home page.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Click Settings">
        The settings pop up — enter your details under **About You**.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Conversations">
    Inventor saves each conversation as a session automatically. Opening the panel starts a new chat, and your past sessions stay available from the **Chats** dropdown in the panel header — browse or search them, then select one to resume where you left off.

    <Info>
      Closing the panel or refreshing the page doesn't delete your conversation history — sessions persist and can be resumed at any time.
    </Info>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Feedback">
    Inventor has built-in feedback tools that help improve its output over time. Each AI message in the panel has thumbs up and thumbs down buttons.

    <CardGroup cols={2}>
      <Card title="Thumbs up" icon="thumbs-up">
        The response was accurate and helpful.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Thumbs down" icon="thumbs-down">
        The response was incorrect, unhelpful, or the generated agent or tool didn't match what you described.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>

    When you click thumbs down, a popup appears where you can submit a bug report directly to the Relevance AI team — without leaving the interface or sending a separate email. Describe what you asked Inventor to build, what it generated, what was wrong or missing, and what you expected instead.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Restore a version">
    Ask Inventor to roll an asset back to an earlier version — for example, "Restore the previous version of this tool". Inventor copies that version into the current draft, leaving the live published version unchanged until you publish. This restores an earlier version of an existing asset; it doesn't recover one that's been deleted.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## MCP vs Inventor

Inventor and the Relevance AI MCP are often compared, but they solve different problems. Inventor builds things _inside_ Relevance AI from natural language. The MCP connects Relevance AI _to the outside world_ — letting external clients call your tools, and letting your agents reach into external services.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Inventor" icon="wand-magic-sparkles">
    A copilot inside Relevance AI. Describe what you want and it builds or edits agents, tools, and workforces for you, landing changes in draft for review.

    Reach for it when you want to build, edit, or debug something inside Relevance AI by describing the outcome.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Relevance AI MCP" icon="plug">
    A bridge to other tools. Call your existing Relevance AI tools from external clients like Cursor or Claude with the [MCP Server](/integrations/mcp/mcp-server), or let your agents call services like Notion and Linear with the [MCP Client](/integrations/mcp/mcp-client).

    Reach for it when the tool already exists and you want to run it from Cursor or Claude, or let an agent call an outside service.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  The two are complementary, not competing. Inventor uses the same MCP tool surface under the hood — so a common pattern is to build your agents and tools with Inventor, then call them from Cursor or Claude through the MCP in your day-to-day work.
</Tip>
